Open decisions

  1. Subsidiary. The Sage exports do not say which entity each customer belongs to: the Entity column was blank on every row, which in Sage usually means the customer is shared at the top level. Every customer is proposed as MinIO, Inc. Which customers bill from MinIO Pvt. Ltd. or MinIO Singapore Pte. Ltd.? A Sage AR invoice report with the Entity column, or accounting, can answer it.
  2. Customer Category. A required NetSuite field that groups customers for reporting. The template allows Direct Customer, Reseller, End Customer and OEM/MSP. Proposed from the Sage customer type: Direct Sales to Direct Customer, Direct Sales with Reseller to End Customer, Reseller to Reseller. These Sage types have no obvious category yet: .
  3. Terms not on the NetSuite list. Map each to a listed term, or add the term in NetSuite. customers have no terms in Sage and are left blank.
  4. Salesforce matches to confirm. customers matched a Salesforce account by a similar but not identical name, for example a spelling or punctuation difference. They are marked "Salesforce match" in Question Categories and listed after the investors. Confirm or reject each.
  5. Parent Company. Salesforce links some accounts to a parent, for example Royal Caribbean International to Royal Caribbean Group. NetSuite can show the same parent and child link, but the parent must exist as a customer. customers have a parent that is not a Sage customer, so it is not in this load. Add those parents to the load as customers, or leave Parent Company blank for them?

Notes

  1. Customer ID. Sage IDs are not consistent. Customers with a Sage ID in the C-##### format keep it. The customers with an ID in another format () get the next numbers after the highest Sage C-##### (): . Their original Sage ID stays in Sage Customer ID.
  2. External ID. Same value as Customer ID, so later loads and updates can find each customer by it.
  3. New customers. After the load, NetSuite should auto-generate customer numbers in the same C-##### format, starting at , the number after the last one in the load. In NetSuite: Setup > Company > Auto-Generated Numbers, Entities subtab, Customer: prefix C-, minimum 5 digits, initial number . Allow Override must be on during the import so the loaded numbers are kept.
  4. Investor records, open question. Sage has active records numbered C00001 to C00016 that look like investors and note holders (for example General Catalyst, Index Ventures, Interest Accrued on Notes), not customers. Should they be loaded into NetSuite as customers? They are in this file, marked "Investor" in Question Categories and listed first. If they are left out, no renumbering is needed and new numbers start right after .
  5. Status. Every customer in this load is active in Sage (not marked inactive), so every customer is set to CUSTOMER-Closed Won. Customers that are renewals can be changed to CUSTOMER-Renewal when deal data is loaded.
  6. Customer Invoice Email. The address invoices go to. Where Sage has an accounts payable or purchasing address (for example ap@, accounts.payable@, invoices@, purchasing@), that address is used; otherwise the first address Sage has. Every other address for the customer goes to Additional Emails.
  7. Reseller billing. For customers Sage invoices through a reseller, the reseller is spelled out in Billed To (Reseller), Reseller Address and Reseller Invoice Email, with Salesforce partners alongside, so it can be set up to best practice.
  8. Terms. Sage terms are mapped to the NetSuite list: Due on Receipt, Net 10, Net 15, Net 30, Net 45, Net 60, Net 150. Sage "30 Days" becomes Net 30, and so on.
  9. Added columns. Question Categories, Sage Customer ID, Salesforce Account ID, Additional Emails and the reseller columns are not standard template columns. Sage Customer ID, Salesforce Account ID and Subnet ID are kept as their own fields so the source of each customer is explicit.
